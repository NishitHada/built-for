Measured this way, inseam closely matches your leg length, so no adjustment is needed. The inseam on a trouser label is usually a little shorter and will understate your legs.

  1. Sit on a hard, flat bench or chair with your back, shoulders and head against a wall. Look straight ahead.
  2. Lay a book flat on your head, touching the wall, and mark the wall under it.
  3. Measure from the seat up to the mark, not from the floor.

Your height minus this number is your leg length, which matters for jumping, running and lifting.

How the scoring works

Advantage

How much more common your build is among a sport’s elite athletes than among people in general. “6.6×” means people with your height and build turn up 6.6 times as often among Olympic badminton players as in the general population. Sports are ranked by this number.

  • 3× or more strong natural advantage
  • 1.5–3× some advantage
  • 0.67–1.5× no clear advantage
  • Below 0.67× built against it

Match

How closely you fit the typical athlete in that sport or position. Being inside the middle half of the athletes on a measurement scores full marks. Match drives the limiting factors in the deep dive.

Where the targets come from

Height and BMI for 27 sports are fitted on Olympic athletes from 1992 to 2016. Arm span, leg, hand, foot and shoulder targets, and sports without Olympic data (climbing, sumo, jockey, coxswain, rugby union, American football, cricket, kabaddi), use published sports-science findings. Team positions are placed around each sport’s real median.

Photo measuring

The pose model runs in your browser and the photo is never uploaded. It finds your joints and converts them to centimetres using your height. Early checks put arm span and leg length within a few centimetres; shoulder width uses a correction from a tape-measure comparison.

Limits

  • Build is one factor among many. Training, skill, aerobic capacity, muscle fibre type, coordination and nerve matter far more for most people, and plenty of champions have the “wrong” body for their sport.
  • “People in general” uses Western adult averages for now, so people from populations with shorter averages are understated in tall sports.
  • Which sports count as popular in each region is an editorial judgement.
  • If you’re still growing, treat the results loosely.

Measure from a photo

Step 1 · Your height
cm
Barefoot. The photo is converted to centimetres using this, so it needs to be accurate.
Step 2 · Take the photo
  1. Wear fitted clothes and stand barefoot against a plain background. Hold your arms straight out to the sides at shoulder height, palms forward, feet slightly apart.
  2. Have someone take the photo from 2–3 m away, straight on, with the camera at waist height. Your whole body, head to feet, must be in the frame.

The photo is analysed on your device and never uploaded. The pose model (about 9 MB) downloads the first time you use it.
