  1. Stand barefoot with your back to a wall and feet about 15 cm apart.
  2. Hold a hardback book level between your legs, spine up, and raise it firmly to your crotch.
  3. Mark the wall at the top of the book and measure from the floor to the mark.

Measured this way, inseam closely matches your leg length, so no adjustment is needed. The inseam on a trouser label is usually a little shorter and will understate your legs.

  1. Sit on a hard, flat bench or chair with your back, shoulders and head against a wall. Look straight ahead.
  2. Lay a book flat on your head, touching the wall, and mark the wall under it.
  3. Measure from the seat up to the mark, not from the floor.

Your height minus this number is your leg length, which matters for jumping, running and lifting.

Straight across your back, from one shoulder’s bony tip to the other.
  1. Find the bony tip of each shoulder: follow your collarbone outwards until it ends in a small bump.
  2. Stand relaxed with arms at your sides and have someone measure across your back from one bump to the other.
  3. Measure in a straight line, not around the curve of your back, and ignore the muscle on the outside of the shoulder.

Typical adults measure about 36 cm (women) or 40 cm (men).

Measure from a photo

  1. Enter your height first. The photo is scaled from it.
  2. Wear fitted clothes and stand barefoot against a plain background. Hold your arms straight out to the sides at shoulder height, palms forward, feet slightly apart.
  3. Have someone take the photo from 2–3 m away, straight on, with the camera at waist height. Your whole body, head to feet, must be in the frame.

The photo is analysed on your device and never uploaded. The pose model (about 9 MB) downloads the first time you use it.

      Estimates are usually within a few centimetres. If one looks wrong, measure it with a tape and type it in.
